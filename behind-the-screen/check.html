How to check information

Before you share something, stop and try these habits.

Read laterally

Leave the unfamiliar website and search for what other sources say about it. Stanford researchers highlight this strategy to gain context and check credibility.

Check where it comes from

Who published it? Look for the author, the date and an "About" page. Be careful with sites that have no signature.

Look for evidence

What supports the claim? Data, documents or experts you can verify are stronger than opinions or screenshots.

Compare with reliable sources

An important story is usually reported by several trustworthy outlets. If only one site has it, be suspicious.

Do not trust looks alone

A professional design does not make a site reliable. Judge the content, not the appearance.

Pause if it makes you angry or afraid

Misleading content often relies on strong emotions. If a message pushes you to forward it, verify first.
