Where to verify

When a claim looks suspicious, check it in places that specialize in verification. Use more than one, and remember that these sites also deserve lateral reading.

Fact-checking organizations

Colombiacheck

Verifies public claims and viral content in Colombia.

Maldita.es

Debunks hoaxes and explains how they were checked (Spanish).

Newtral

Fact-checking journalism in Spanish.

AFP Fact Check

Verification from a global news agency, in several languages.

Reuters Fact Check

Checks viral claims, images and videos (English).

Snopes

One of the oldest sites for rumors and urban legends (English).

FactCheck.org

Nonpartisan project that checks claims in U.S. politics (English).

Tools to search and verify

Google Fact Check Explorer

Search a claim and see the fact-checks that other organizations published.

TinEye

Reverse image search to find where else a picture appeared and when.

Google Lens

Search with an image to find its origin or similar photos.

Learn how to verify

UNESCO: Media and Information Literacy

Skills to access, analyze and evaluate information responsibly.

Stanford Report: Judging fact from fiction online

Explains lateral reading and how to check credibility.

IFCN Code of Principles

Lists fact-checkers that commit to standards of transparency and fairness.

Tip: search the claim plus the words "fact check" or "verificación" to find existing checks quickly.
